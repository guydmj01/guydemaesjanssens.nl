My story

From host to tech lead

I didn't study computer science. I come from hospitality. Looking back, that's what makes the difference.

  1. Achterhoek · The Hague

    The host

    I grew up in the Achterhoek, in the east of the Netherlands, and studied business administration at Hotel School The Hague. All those years I worked in hospitality. It teaches you one thing well: think about what your guest needs next. People are with you in their free time, so you make it their best time.

  2. Germany

    Data before IT

    For my graduation I worked for a large German hotel chain. I built a pricing model for several of their hotels, based on past bookings for rooms and events, plus holidays, local events and the weather. It let them adjust their prices every day. That's when I knew: I wanted the technical side, but right in the middle of the business.

  3. 2018 – 2022

    Starting on the shop floor

    An IT traineeship placed me at Rabobank. After a year the bank asked me to join them, in their Young Professional Programme. I started on the service deskThe IT help desk: the people employees call when their computer or software doesn't work., moved to global hardwareThe physical equipment: laptops, screens, phones and the servers in data centres. delivery and became scrum masterA coach for a team that works in short cycles of a few weeks. They keep the way of working on track and clear away what slows the team down. They don't manage the work itself. for several infrastructure teams. That's how I got to know IT the way users and teams really experience it.

  4. 2022 – now

    Selected to lead

    I was selected from a small group of high potentials for the bank's Tech Lead programme. Since 2022 I've been a tech leadThe person responsible for the technical direction and quality of one or more teams, and for the people in them.: first for the digital workplace of about 10,000 Rabobank employees, now for the platformThe shared foundation of tools, building blocks and rules that software teams build on. Like a well-equipped workshop everyone can use. more than 200 DevOps teamsTeams that both build software and keep it running. No hand-over to a separate department: the same people own it from start to finish. build on.

    My success doesn't come from being the best at what I do. It comes from building teams that are.

    About my work as a tech lead

  5. At home

    Learning by building

    Because I didn't study computer science, I put in extra time to really understand the technology. I do that by building. First home automation, then our home's energy use, now mostly AI.

    I built a golf coach app that gives feedback on a video of your swing. There are no AI models for golf swings, so I used a large pose modelAI that recognises a person's posture in a photo or video, as a stick figure of joints and limbs. as the teacher and trained a smaller modelTraining a small, fast AI model by letting it learn from a big, slow one. Like an apprentice learning from a master. that gives concrete feedback. And at home I run my own AI lab with local AIAI models that run on your own computers instead of at a cloud provider. Your data never leaves the building.: good enough for real work, without any data going to the big cloud providers.
